Upcoming: 1.1.0#

The dev branch is at version 1.1.0. The entries below are merged into dev but not yet released.

Fixes that were also released in 1.0.11 are listed under that version as well.

Features:

  • Double precision on Apple Metal: the device mps with precision: float64 (it was refused before) keeps the data as float64 on the host and runs the Fourier multislice (also the core-loss one), the infinite-projection potential and the large host FFTs on the GPU in double-float arithmetic (pairs of float32, ~1e-14 relative accuracy), up to ~16x faster than float64 on the CPU. What the GPU cannot do (real-space multislice, a grid side with a prime factor above 7) runs on the host in the same float64, with a SlowFFTSizeWarning; torch.emulate-float64 asks for the same with the device cpu. A SlowFFTSizeWarning also says when a grid is markedly slow on Metal, whose FFT runs powers of two 2-4x faster per pixel, and which grid would be faster; the automatic grids stay the same on every device. The test suite now runs its double-precision tests on Metal at the same tolerances as elsewhere (PR #620)

  • Bloch waves on a Mac in double precision: bloch.solver: auto takes the band solver (see solver='band') over the eigendecomposition of the structure matrix from 500 beams for float64 matrices on macOS (other platforms keep the eigendecomposition). On the host the band reduction now runs on the lower triangle only, through the Hermitian matrix product and rank-2k update of the BLAS that SciPy is built with (called with a leading dimension, so nothing is copied), and the Chebyshev recurrence is a compiled kernel, with a bandwidth of 32 instead of 128. A quartz rotation series of 1000 Å took 0.23 s a frame at 1299 beams instead of 0.69 s, 0.79 s instead of 3.5 s at 2195 beams and 2.1 s instead of 11.3 s at 3233 beams on an Apple M2 (against zheevr, see the fix above), with intensities within 1e-12 of the eigendecomposition. With device: mps and precision: float64 (or torch.emulate-float64) the matrix of 3000 beams or more is reduced on the GPU instead, by double-float matrix products (a lean multiply-add, 160 GFLOP/s on the M2, with the panels and block reflectors in float64 on the host) and the recurrence runs on the GPU for 1600 beams or more: 1.1 to 1.3x faster than the host band solver from 3000 to 9000 beams (33 against 42 s at 9009), with intensities within 3e-12 (100 Å) to 4e-11 (2000 Å) of the eigendecomposition at 1299 beams, against 5e-14 to 6e-13 on the host (PR #700, PR #702)

  • CrystalPotential(..., correct_surfaces=...) calculates the slices at the entrance and exit surfaces of the crystal as they are in a slab, by default for a unit with a finite projection. Without it the crystal is a periodic stack, whose first and last slices hold the finite-projection potential of the images of the unit beyond the surfaces (atoms a slab does not have; with an atomic plane on the slice boundary the last slice differed from a slab’s by as much as its largest slice potential) and miss the tails of the potential of the surface atoms beyond them. With it, the slices within the integration cutoff of a surface are calculated without the images, and the tails beyond the surface are added to the slice next to it, which conserves the projected potential of the slab; the slices equal those of a slab with vacuum, with the vacuum slices added to the adjacent ones. The crystal itself is unchanged: its slices, box, thickness and exit_planes are as without, so nothing shifts and the exit wave is not propagated further. The tails are at the depth of the outermost slice rather than their own, so the error falls with thinner slices and with the distance of the atoms from the entrance and exit plane of the unit. For Si at 100 kV, the diffraction intensity of a plane wave is closer to that of the slab than that of the periodic stack by a factor 2.9 for atoms on the plane and 7 at 0.3 Å from it on four slices per unit (5.6 and 14 on eight), and the ADF at the columns of a 20 mrad probe (50-100 mrad) is within 0.4 % of the slab’s from 0.3 Å on. For atoms on the plane the correction is poor: the ADF at their columns is too high by 12 % for Si on four slices per unit (5 % on eight) and by 10 % and 38 % for Au on quarter-lattice and half-lattice slices, against 28-37 % too low for the periodic stack of Si, so a warning is given for atoms of the unit within 0.3 Å of the plane, which suggests centering them in the cell with atoms.center(axis=2). Behaviour change: the first and last slices of a CrystalPotential of a finite-projection unit differ from those of the periodic stack; pass correct_surfaces=False for the previous ones. A CrystalPotential that stacks a unit along z raises if a CrystalPotential at any depth of its units corrects its surfaces (pass correct_surfaces=False to it); a built crystal (a PotentialArray from build()) is a slab with no flag to check, and must not be stacked along z. An infinite projection has no surfaces to correct, and a unit that is not a Potential is left as it is (PR #648)

  • Energy ensemble support across the codebase: PlaneWave, Probe, SMatrix, BlochWaves and CTF accept a list of energies, and the resulting EnergyAxis propagates through indexing, angular sampling, unit conversion and diffraction-spot indexing (PR #257)

    • Bloch-wave rotation ensembles: BlochWaves(..., energy=[...]).rotate(axis, angles) with a distribution of angles computes every energy. Diffraction patterns and exit waves get an EnergyAxis after the rotation axes, the axis order of a multislice rotation series, and each energy and orientation keeps its own beams within the union beam set (PR #521)

  • C-PRISM: SMatrix(upsample=True) reduces every probe from the complete plane-wave expansion of the aperture, so the interpolation factor only sets the number of multislice runs. Adds CompressedSMatrixArray and GridScan.commensurate (PR #318)

  • Phonon-loss (thermal diffuse scattering) energy-loss workflow: EnergyResolvedAtomsEnsemble, phonon_loss_diffraction_patterns, momentum_resolved_spectrum and MomentumResolvedSpectrum, the SpectralAnnularDetector and SpectralSlitDetector, and detailed-balance thermal weighting that splits the classical TDS signal into loss and gain sides (PR #324, PR #351)

  • elastic_diffuse_diffraction_patterns (also a Waves method) forms the total, elastic and diffuse intensity of any exit waves with a frozen-phonon axis, keeping every other ensemble axis. It has unbiased=True for the \((N - 1)/N\) bias of the diffuse part and reduction_dtype="float64" for diffuse signals much weaker than the total. phonon_loss_diffraction_patterns now builds on it and gains the same options. Its keyword component becomes components, and the components "incoherent", "coherent" and "tds" become "total", "elastic" and "diffuse"; the old names raise a ValueError naming the new one. "all" stacks ("total", "elastic", "diffuse"), a tuple or list of names stacks them in the order given, and the metadata key phonon_loss_component becomes frozen_phonon_component. The loss/gain unfolding refuses a bool, negative or non-finite temperature; at zero the whole signal is on the loss side. momentum_resolved_spectrum takes the diffuse component from a stacked result and raises a ValueError for the total or elastic component, which contain the Bragg intensity (PR #484)

  • Linear-scaling PRISM-EELS for core-loss simulations: SMatrix.transition_potential_scan, with single- and double-channel scattering and an optional windowed inelastic crop (PR #289)

    • BiP-PRISM beam partitioning for PRISM-EELS: transition_potential_scan(reduction="beam_basis") uses a dual-scattering-matrix driver. partitions_s1 and partitions_s2 build S1 and S2 on sparse hex-ring parent beams and reconstruct the full beam set at each atom by natural-neighbor interpolation. collection_angle limits S2 to the detector disk, and focal_backprop is an optional accuracy lever for S1. Options that would have no effect raise a ValueError (PR #338)

  • In addition to the Gaussian (G) distribution, now also implemented Lorentzian (L), Voigtian (convolution L * G) and pseudo-Voigtian (L + G) source-size distributions and filters (PR #270)

  • The exact free-space propagator is now the default for Fourier multislice, FourierMultislice(order="exact"), replacing the paraxial approximation. Spatial frequencies beyond k > 1 / lambda are treated as evanescent rather than propagating; the paraxial propagator remains available as order=1 (PR #298)

  • Magnetic potentials and fields from collinear GPAW calculations: gpaw_magnetic_fields builds the electrostatic potential, vector potential and magnetic field from the same calculator(s) in one call, returning a GPAWMagneticFields bundle with .tile(), .combined_potential() and .show(); rotate_field now defaults to "auto" (PR #326)

  • GPAWParametrization is now usable: it fits a Lobato-form IAM potential to the X-ray scattering factor of an all-electron GPAW calculation, with working ionization support and a regularized fit (PR #329)

  • Potential(sampling="auto", slice_thickness="auto") automatically finds a grid commensurate with the atomic lattice — so translation-equivalent atoms discretize identically — near the usual default targets (\(0.05 \ \mathrm{Å}\), \(1 \ \mathrm{Å}\)), preferring FFT-fast grid sizes wherever that is compatible with commensurability. New grid.round-to-fast-fft config key ('auto'/True/False), Grid.round_to_fast_fft(), and is_fast_fft_size/next_fast_fft_size helpers in abtem.core.fft (PR #274, PR #347)

  • Significant improvements on simulating large potentials on GPU, alongside minor performance improvements (PR #269)

    • The potential is now built in chunks of contiguous slices instead of all at once, keeping peak VRAM bounded; new config key potential.slice-chunk-size (default "auto")

    • Opt-in multi-GPU via the new config key dask.multi-gpu (requires dask-cuda)

    • cupy.fft-cache-size’s previous 0 MB default silently disabled the cuFFT plan cache; changed to -1 (unlimited) here, then to the device-relative auto default below once unbounded retention turned out to cost tens of GB on FFT-unfriendly grids

  • Bloch waves default to the non-paraxial wave equation, use_wave_eq="exact", the counterpart of the default exact multislice propagator, FourierMultislice(order="exact") (PR #447, PR #448). use_wave_eq=True remains the paraxial counterpart of order=1, and use_wave_eq=False the standard (textbook) Bloch-wave equation, which was the default before

    • Results computed with default arguments change. For Si with sg_max=0.5, the R factor between the old and new defaults stays below 1 % at 100–300 keV, but reaches 5 % at 20 keV on zone axis and 24 % at 20 keV, 1000 Å thick and 7° off zone axis. Passing use_wave_eq=False does not restore the old numbers exactly, because of the metric fix under Bugfixes

    • get_kinematical_diffraction_pattern weights each reflection with the excitation error of the selected form, so kinematical patterns also change slightly off zone axis. Beam selection (sg_max) and diffraction-spot indexing keep the Ewald-sphere excitation error

    • With "exact", beams with \(\lambda |g_\perp| \geq 1\) cannot propagate; BlochWaves excludes them with a warning. This happens only at low energies with a large g_max (it needs \(|g| \approx \sqrt{2}/\lambda\), about \(3.6 \ \mathrm{Å^{-1}}\) at 1 keV)

  • BlochWaves.calculate_scattering_matrix(z, lazy=True) returns a dask array; the method also no longer fails on GPU (PR #448)

  • Bloch waves follow the precision setting like the rest of abTEM, so the default is now single precision; set precision="float64" for the previous double-precision results (PR #449). Single precision is accurate to about \(2 \times 10^{-4}\) relative for beams down to \(10^{-3}\) of the strongest, even at 10–20 kÅ, and any single-precision Bloch-wave propagation emits a BlochWavePrecisionWarning recommending a double-precision check. The scattering matrix (calculate_scattering_matrix) is always exponentiated in double precision internally, as scaling and squaring breaks down in single precision for realistic beam counts

  • Multi-energy BlochWaves: excitation_errors() returns one row per energy over the union beam set, get_kinematical_diffraction_pattern() returns patterns with an EnergyAxis, and the new select_energy(energy) returns the single-energy Bloch waves; calculate_structure_matrix and calculate_scattering_matrix still need a single energy and point to it (PR #456)

  • Convergent-beam electron diffraction from Bloch waves: BlochWaves.calculate_cbed_patterns solves each plane wave of a probe and places its beams on the probe’s grid. Without a scan it gives the position-averaged (PACBED) pattern; with one, the plane waves add coherently at each position, so overlapping discs interfere. Aberration, aperture and tilt series (precession included) and energy ensembles are supported, and the calculation is lazy. BlochwaveEnsemble takes a fixed orientation_matrix (PR #254)

  • The GPU test runner tools/gpu/run_gpu_tests.sh has ABTEM_CI_OFFLINE=1, which tests the managed clone as it is, without clone, fetch or reset, and installs from uv’s cache only, for compute nodes without internet access. On ROCm it runs one pytest process per test file, reports the passed and the failed or errored tests, detects aborts and fails when no test passed. ABTEM_CI_DESELECT takes a pytest -k expression of tests to leave out of the sweep, and the status.tsv summary names it. The recorded commit gets a -dirty suffix when tracked files differ from HEAD (PR #708)

Performance:

  • Multislice of an ensemble of atoms (such as a 3DED rotation series) no longer repeats host work for every chunk or exit plane, and its potential needs fewer FFTs

    • The atoms of an ensemble are sliced once per call of generate_chunked_slices instead of once per chunk

    • Waves.window keeps its taper, and the power gain of a window is computed once, instead of both building an n x n array on the host at every exit plane

    • ScatteringFactorProjectionIntegrals builds its sinc once per grid instead of for every slice and element

    • The default (infinite) projection transforms the real slices of the potential with rfft2 and irfft2, half the work and memory of the complex transforms, with fft="numpy", on a GPU and with torch (CPU and Metal; the wrapper allocates the output, which avoids a PyTorch warning of the Metal kernels about resizing it); this one agrees with the complex transforms to rounding (half an epsilon of the largest value) and not bit for bit; the elements of a slice are summed as half spectra and transformed back once

    • Measured with benchmarks/bench_real_fft_potential.py on an Apple silicon laptop (Metal, single precision), the potential of the default projection builds 1.22x (1024 x 1024, 8 slices), 1.31x (2048 x 2048, 8 slices), 1.62x (4096 x 4096, 16 slices) and 1.67x (2048 x 2048, 80 slices) faster with the real transforms

    • Potential.generate_slices no longer finds the elements of all the atoms at every chunk

    • Measured for one orientation of a py3DED run (160 x 160 x 1000 Å, sampling 0.02 Å, slices of 0.4 Å, an 8000 x 8000 grid, 251 exit planes) on a 24 GB RTX 4090 in single precision: monoclinic ZrO2 takes 91.1 s instead of 199.6 s (2.19x) and kyanite 104.5 s instead of 253.0 s (2.42x). The patterns of the first three changes are bit-identical; with the real transforms they differ from the old ones by 4e-6 of the largest value, about a tenth of the error of the single precision run itself against a double precision one (4.6e-5 and 8.9e-5), which they leave unchanged

    • Found by benchmarks/bench_multislice_3ded_payload.py, which splits one orientation of a py3DED multislice run into stages, and measured commit by commit with benchmarks/bisect_multislice_gains.py

  • The projection integrator is shared by reference across ensemble members instead of being deep-copied (and re-uploaded to the GPU) for each (PR #350)

  • Removed a redundant potential rebuild on every scan chunk (PR #340)

  • CrystalPotential builds its unit once per simulation instead of once per ensemble member and lazy scan block, unless every member reseeds its own frozen-phonon pool; a list of three .gpw files with six members is read 3 times instead of 18 (PR #524)

  • The automatic batch sizes of PRISM count the exit planes: the plane waves per chunk of SMatrix.build hold every exit plane within dask.chunk-size, and the positions per reduction batch of SMatrixArray.reduce, SMatrix.reduce and SMatrix.scan hold the reduced waves of every exit plane and ensemble member. The peak memory of an eager scan with 9 exit planes on a 128 x 128 grid falls from 9.2 GB to 1.3 GB; an integer max_batch*, and a potential with one exit plane and no ensemble, are batched as before. Where a batch changes, values move by float32 rounding of the reduction only (PR #563)

  • More automatic batch sizes stay within the configured chunk size. Where a batch changes, values move by float32 rounding of the reduction only; an integer max_batch or max_batch_reduction is batched as before (PR #649)

    • Probe.multislice, Probe.scan, Probe.transition_potential_scan and PlaneWave.multislice size the automatic max_batch for the output of a block, which holds one result per exit plane and detector for each of its waves: a block of exit waves or diffraction patterns was that many times larger than dask.chunk-size (18 times with 23 exit planes on a 128 x 128 grid). The batch is divided by the output of the block in units of one wave when that exceeds one wave: detectors whose outputs together exceed one wave per position also shrink the batch of a potential with a single exit plane, while for realistic grid sizes and plane counts integrating detectors such as AnnularDetector and FlexibleAnnularDetector are batched as before

    • The automatic max_batch_reduction of SMatrixArray.reduce, SMatrix.reduce, SMatrix.scan and CompressedSMatrixArray.reduce is sized with dask.chunk-size-gpu on a GPU, where it used dask.chunk-size on every device

    • The automatic max_batch_reduction of an interpolated PRISM reduction (a window smaller than the S-matrix grid) also counts the box that the batch is contracted over, which spans the batch and one window: the contracted array was 1.7 times the chunk size at dask.chunk-size="1 MB" and 5.8 times at "16 MB" in a 44 x 44 scan, and is now below the chunk size

  • BlochWaves.calculate_exit_waves sums the plane-wave expansion as two matrix products, using that the beams are separable in x, y and z, instead of forming and reducing the (thickness, beam, x, y) array of plane waves. For 479 beams, 5 thicknesses and a 256 x 256 grid the synthesis takes 6 ms instead of 256 ms, and its peak memory 19 MB instead of 5 GB; values agree to float64 rounding (PR #630)

  • The Bloch-wave structure matrix is gathered on the device from the structure factors on a dense grid, instead of being looked up through pandas on the host from the (beams², 3) array of Miller index differences; on the GPU that lookup took two thirds of the time of a rotation series at 13,903 beams. The eigendecomposition reuses the structure matrix’s memory where the backend allows (CuPy, and NumPy in double precision), and the beam amplitudes no longer form two more beams x beams arrays (PR #635)

    • A centrosymmetric crystal is solved as a real symmetric eigenproblem, that of the crystal moved to its inversion center, which halves the memory of the structure matrix and its eigenvectors and is faster to factorize; results agree with the complex solve to rounding. StructureFactor(inversion_center="auto") detects the center from the atoms, and inversion_center=None keeps the complex solve

    • Measured for one orientation of monoclinic ZrO2 (13,903 beams, float32) on a 24 GB RTX 4090: the structure matrix takes 0.08 s instead of 10 s and the eigendecomposition 2.8 s instead of 5.7 s, with a peak of 3.9 GB instead of 9.3 GB (2.5 instead of 6 complex beams x beams matrices); the error against double precision is 2.6e-4 instead of 4.3e-4. Kyanite’s 28,898 beams, which ran out of memory, fit with a peak of 16.7 GB

  • Bloch waves have a second solver, chosen by the configuration key bloch.solver: "band" reduces the structure matrix in place to band form with matrix products and expands the incident wave in Chebyshev polynomials of the band matrix, instead of diagonalizing it ("eigh"). The default "auto" uses it on a GPU when it is estimated to be faster, which is for thousands of beams; on the CPU, and for small or very thick crystals, the eigendecomposition is faster (PR #642)

    • Measured for one orientation on a 24 GB RTX 4090 in single precision, with thicknesses to 1000 Å: monoclinic ZrO2 (13,903 beams) takes 0.71 s instead of 2.90 s (real symmetric form) and 1.13 s instead of 5.77 s (complex), and kyanite (28,898 beams) 2.56 s instead of 24.3 s. The peak memory is a quarter (0.5-0.7 instead of 2.5 complex beams x beams matrices, 1.1-1.3 instead of 5.0), so kyanite’s complex form fits (7.2 GB). The error against double precision is 4.8e-5, 9.4e-5 and 9.7e-5 instead of 2.6e-4, 4.3e-4 and 1.3e-3: the eigendecomposition’s grows with the number of beams

    • The expansion costs about 0.3 s per 1000 Å of the largest thickness on that GPU

    • A rotation series computes the structure factors once, not once per orientation or dask block

  • Behaviour change: OMP_NUM_THREADS caps the threads of Numba’s parallel=True kernels and of the interpolation pool of the finite-projection potential when NUMBA_NUM_THREADS is unset and OMP_NUM_THREADS is a positive integer, where 1.0.11 used every CPU usable by the process for Numba and every CPU of the machine for the interpolation pool (PR #658)

    • With abtem imported before numba, the cap is the size of Numba’s whole thread pool, so it holds for every parallel=True kernel in the process, the application’s own included, and the size of the interpolation pool. With numba imported first, Numba’s pool and its configuration are unchanged and the cap applies to the interpolation pool and, set on the launching thread for the duration of each launch, to the real-space Laplacian stencil (RealSpaceMultislice) only. On a 24-CPU machine with OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 and abtem imported first, 1.0.11 gave Numba and the interpolation pool 24 threads and both now have 1. OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 is the default of many HPC schedulers and the value a dask nanny sets in its worker processes when the variable is unset, so these kernels then run on one thread. An explicit NUMBA_NUM_THREADS overrides the cap

    • The cap is at most the number of CPUs usable by the process (os.sched_getaffinity, else os.cpu_count())

  • Behaviour change: the interpolation pool of the finite-projection potential has NUMBA_NUM_THREADS threads when it is set and otherwise the cap above or, without one, Numba’s default, the number of usable CPUs, where 1.0.11 used os.cpu_count() whatever the process may use: under taskset -c 0-2 the pool has 3 threads where it had 24 on a 24-CPU machine, and with NUMBA_NUM_THREADS=5 it has 5 where it had 24. Potentials built with different interpolation thread counts differ in the last float32 bits: for a silicon cubic cell repeated 3 x 3 x 3 with gpts=128 and finite projection, two processes with 24 and with 4 interpolation threads differed by up to 4.3e-7 of the maximum (PR #658)

  • An eager SMatrix.build, and so an eager SMatrix.reduce or SMatrix.scan, of a CrystalPotential whose members reseed their frozen-phonon pools (seeds or num_frozen_phonons) builds each member’s pool once instead of once per plane-wave block: 3 members over 14 blocks build 3 pools instead of 42, and an eager reduce at a dask.chunk-size of 16 kB builds 3 instead of 165. The pool is held while the member is built and released when its build returns; values are unchanged (PR #666)

Dependencies:

  • The core-loss extra is merged into a single gpaw = ["hankel", "sympy"] extra, and a new all extra installs every optional runtime dependency (PR #329)

  • The mps extra requires torch 2.14 (was 2.4). On torch 2.10 to 2.13, Metal has no linalg.eigh, and an FFT over an axis before the last four (such as gaussian_source_size of a scan with a further ensemble axis) aborts the process. Selecting the mps device with an older torch already installed now raises a RuntimeError asking for an upgrade, instead of aborting later; upgrade with pip install -U torch

Bugfixes:

  • A lazy DiffractionPatterns.integrate_radial and azimuthal_average of integer counts declare the dtype they compute. integrate_radial declared the integer dtype of the counts but summed to int64, which is now the declared and computed dtype (floating point counts keep theirs; with a sensitivity the result has the configured precision). azimuthal_average declared the integer dtype but computed float64 whatever the configured precision, and with weighting_function="gaussian" truncated the averages to integers; the averages are now in the configured precision, and floating point counts keep their dtype. The step bins of azimuthal_average summed small integer counts (uint8, int16, uint16) in their own type, which wraps around, so a uniform pattern averaged to a different value in every bin; they are summed in the configured precision, which the GPU kernel needs too (it only reads floating point values) (PR #695)

This affected Images.interpolate(method="spline"), interpolate_line and interpolate_line_at_position of Images and DiffractionPatterns (so also Probe.profiles and momentum_resolved_spectrum with a SpectralSlitDetector), and RealSpaceLineProfiles.interpolate and ReciprocalSpaceLineProfiles.interpolate. On CuPy, the prefilter, the index and the weights are now computed in double precision and the sum in the precision of the data, so the results agree with the NumPy results to float32 rounding. On earlier CuPy versions the results change by float32 rounding only. Behaviour change: at the default float32 precision, the pixel positions of the line in interpolate_line and interpolate_line_at_position were Å values rounded to float32 (by up to 3.8e-5 pixels at 0.1 Å sampling near 100 Å). On NumPy and CuPy they are now built in float64 (LineScan.get_positions takes an optional dtype, by default the configured precision as before), which changes the values of these functions and of what builds on them (Probe.profiles, momentum_resolved_spectrum) slightly, by 5e-6 to 4e-5 of the maximum for random data in the cases checked; at float64 precision they are unchanged. Metal keeps float32 pixel positions, so its line values shift by float32 noise (up to 7e-5 of the maximum in the cases checked) without becoming more accurate. The results of Images.interpolate, RealSpaceLineProfiles.interpolate and ReciprocalSpaceLineProfiles.interpolate on NumPy, Metal and float64 CuPy are unchanged (PR #680)

It no longer writes into an axes list the object shares with its caller or with other objects, so a list read from ensemble_axes_metadata before the call keeps the old axes

With 4 visible GPUs and two devices listed, the workers shared 42.5 % of the memory limit in total, and now share 85 %. More devices listed than visible, four listed with two visible, gave 170 %

An asynchronous client inside its event loop, whose query returns a coroutine, is not used: the computation runs on the synchronous scheduler, where the check raised TypeError

  • shrink_cell works under python -O

  • The Miller-index labels of an indexed diffraction-pattern plot delegate only the methods of matplotlib’s Annotation: hasattr, getattr with a default and copy.deepcopy work on them, and any other name raises AttributeError

  • The warning of the GPAW all-electron fallback names the assertion that failed (its source line, message and location), where it reported a convergence failure for any assertion

  • Indexing an ensemble axis with a step, a negative start or stop, a reversed slice or an index array keeps the coordinates of the selected members. The axis kept the unsliced offset and sampling, so dp[::2, ::2] of a 4D-STEM scan reported the full scan’s sampling, and the images derived from it half their true size. A partial slice of a ReciprocalSpaceAxis with fftshift=False kept the class, whose coordinates for the shorter length are not those of the selected members of an axis stored in ifftshift order (PR #696)

    • Behaviour change: a reversed slice of a scan axis (dp[::-1]), or an index array whose members decrease evenly, lists the same positions from the last. The axis stays a scan axis, with the first selected position as its offset and a negative sampling. Before, dp[::-1] reversed the data and kept the axes, so its reductions were mirrored. Reductions of a scan to images or line profiles return the positions in increasing order, with a positive sampling, so dp[::-1] and dp give the same images: integrate_radial, center_of_mass, integrated_center_of_mass, PolarMeasurements.integrate, differentials and to_image_ensemble, and the detectors that reduce the scan of waves (AnnularDetector and the spectral detectors), multislice included. Every other output keeps the scan in the order it was indexed, whatever else the transform returns: the detectors that keep the scan as ensemble axes (PixelatedDetector, FlexibleAnnularDetector, SegmentedDetector, WavesDetector), alone or next to a reducing detector in one multislice, the transforms of waves (apply_ctf, multislice exit waves), and the operations on measurements (the source-size filters, poisson_noise). The filters and poisson_noise with dose_per_area use the distance between neighbouring positions. A scan of waves that are already scanned (Waves.scan) puts its own positions in increasing order and keeps the scan of the waves as it was indexed. A single position selected through a reversal (dp[::-1][:1], dp[3:2:-1]) has a positive sampling and is not a reversed scan. A reversed slice of another linear axis, and an index array on one, give a NonLinearAxis with the selected coordinates, and so do a slice with a step other than one of a ReciprocalSpaceAxis and any slice of one with fftshift=False that does not select every member. On a scan axis, an index array whose members are evenly spaced is the equivalent slice (dp[[0, 2, 4]] is dp[0:5:2]) and keeps the scan axis. Any other index array gives a NonLinearAxis there too, so the axis is no longer a scan axis: reductions over it return line profiles instead of Images for a 2D scan, and a MeasurementsEnsemble instead of line profiles for a 1D scan; integrated_center_of_mass, the source-size filters (gaussian_source_size, lorentzian_source_size, voigtian_source_size and pseudo_voigtian_source_size, of diffraction patterns and of polar measurements) and poisson_noise with dose_per_area raise when one of the two scan axes was indexed so; and a reduction of dp[:, [1, 2, 4]] puts the indexed axis first. Outer indexing of linear ensemble axes (np.ix_), or any index array of more than one dimension on one, raises IndexError.

The message names the first differing axis, the fields that differ and both values (for example offset 0.25 vs 2.25). They gave every member the axes of the first one, so the others silently got its coordinates. The RuntimeError of concatenate for an axis that cannot be joined now carries a message

  • get_items(index, keepdims=True) with a negative integer index returns the member counted from the end of the axis, and an index out of range raises IndexError. It returned an empty array and an empty axis. A numpy integer index (np.int64(2)) keeps a length-1 axis like an int; it returned an array without the ensemble axis

Eager patterns of ensemble shape (3,) with matrices of ensemble shape (3, 1) or (3, 1, 1), and (4, 3) with (4, 1, 1) or (4, 1, 1, 1), for example, were indexed with the right matrices

1.0.11#

A bugfix release on top of 1.0.10 with fixes ported from dev; no features and no new defaults. Plain potentials, plane-wave and probe multislice with default settings, ensembles of unweighted parameters, zarr round trips and the configuration defaults are bit-identical to 1.0.10 at the default float32 precision (checked on 66 scenarios).

Fixed: results that change#

Magnitudes are from small test systems and vary with the data.

4D-STEM and measurements

  • Centre of mass and iCOM are divided by the captured intensity (#417); an all-zero pattern gives NaN instead of 0.

  • Default diffraction patterns are unshifted: their coordinates, azimuthal_average, bandlimit and centre of mass now agree with that (#463; azimuthal averages differ by up to 80 %).

  • Radial bins are exactly step_size wide (#469; default ADF/HAADF move by 0.2 to 0.8 %, radial_binning by 13 to 33 %).

  • A FlexibleAnnularDetector without outer follows the waves of each call, not the first ones it saw (#434).

  • PolarMeasurements.integrate azimuthal limits (#459), rad/deg conversions (#458, off by 1e6), depth_profile and Images.coordinates (#461), interpolate_line and 2 / images (#470), periodic spline interpolation (#506).

  • AnnularDetector(offset=...) is applied (#324); get_detector_region returns percent as a float (#283).

  • block_direct=True blocks the whole bright-field disk instead of 1 mrad (#484); block_direct() without cutoff blocks only the zero-angle pixel (#556).

  • Reciprocal-space waves measure like real-space ones (#565, #566); lazy gaussian_source_size is periodic like the eager one (#485); integrate_gradient takes the minimum per image (#556).

  • Seeded Poisson and scan noise give another realisation for the same seed (#464, #475). Float32 stays float32 in transfer functions, integrate_gradient and center_of_mass (#604).

  • PixelatedDetector(reciprocal_space=False) has real-space axes, WavesDetector(gpts=...) declares the right shape, and block_direct no longer grows its metadata (#499).

PRISM

  • Aberrated SMatrix.reduce/scan match Probe (#318; 17 to 75 %). An eager frozen-phonon reduce is averaged like the lazy one (#576), and eager SMatrix.scan/reduce reduce the CTF ensemble axes (#466). PRISM no longer leaves its outer angle on the caller’s detector (#491).

Potentials and atoms

  • orthogonalize_cell followed by Potential no longer drops atoms near cell faces (#345, #390, #555; wurtzite GaN lost 2 of 8 atoms, projected potential +33 %). Non-periodic cells keep displaced atoms (#460, #555).

  • CrystalPotential honours first_slice/last_slice (#269); GPAWPotential(repetitions=...) was wrong by about 100 % (#494); GPAWParametrization applies charge (#329).

  • GPAW and Bloch slices are integrated centred on their slices, which shifts each slice by dz/2 (5 % for GPAW, 17 to 30 % for Bloch; whole-cell sums unchanged) (#574); ChargeDensityPotential slices are sampled at midpoints (#472).

  • IAM magnetic fields change by ~1e-6 and their slices are labelled ÅT, Å²T (#543); projection="finite" with sigmas for only some elements dropped the others (#480); the GPAW magnetic real_space projection ignored first_slice (#544); wrapped_crop_2d outside the cell (#389); eager multislice of potentials with two or more ensemble axes scrambled the results (#333).

Bloch waves

  • Non-centrosymmetric structures were computed for the negated structure (#379; wurtzite exit waves change by up to 1.27).

  • The symmetrising metric is applied consistently (#448): the eigendecomposition and matrix-exponential paths now agree to rounding. For Si at 100 keV, default intensities change by R = 1.5e-3 to 2.2e-3 on the zone axis and 3e-4 to 1.3e-3 at a 7° tilt (R = Σ|ΔI| / ΣI).

  • Eager rotation ensembles (#450), A/B/C centring (#487) and CuPy scattering matrices (#449).

HRTEM and CTF

  • set_aberrations('scherzer') C10 sign and ordering (#471); apply_ctf no longer writes the waves’ energy into the CTF (#489).

Core-loss

  • Continuum normalisation (#364), the (2l+1) double count (#368; L2,3 ÷3, M4,5 ÷5), site threshold and a spurious 1.02 factor (#468; K +3 to 4 %, L2,3 −22 to −27 %) and the vxc vacuum tail (−4 % at 3 eV). Single-channel frozen-phonon core loss was num_configs times too large or zero (#387).

Backscattering

  • Back-propagation is corrected (#562) and ensembles work (#499).

Weighted distributions

  • Distribution weights are probabilities applied when the ensemble is reduced (#466). In 1.0.10 a STEM focal spread was effectively a top-hat, an HRTEM Gaussian spread had a standard deviation of σ/√2, and tilt and aperture weights were ignored. The peak of the reduced intensity of a 200 kV, 25 mrad probe with gaussian(σ, num_samples=11) defocus is 1.76 times the 1.0.10 value at σ = 25 Å and 2.21 times at 50 Å. Frozen-phonon results are unchanged.

  • New: OrdinalAxis.weights/from_distribution and reduce_ensemble(axis=). gaussian weights sum to one; normalize='intensity' and 'amplitude' are deprecated; .sum()/.mean() over a weighted axis warn; negative or wrong-length weights raise ValueError. Bloch rotation spreads and noise axes carry no weights, as before. Zarr files with a weighted axis cannot be read by 1.0.10.

Distributed computations

  • The client’s configuration reaches the workers of a distributed client, also for to_tiff and to_zarr(compute=False) (#346). Workers used to see only the YAML defaults, so for example precision='float64' runs were computed in float32. Distributed results obtained with a non-default configuration are worth repeating.

Other

  • FFTW fftn over three axes transformed the wrong axes and strided input with overwrite_x=True raised (#558); a lazy fft2_convolve had the wrong shape for long kernels (#577).

  • Waves.phase_shift relabelled reciprocal-space waves and widened complex64 (#550), as did tile(renormalize=True) (#414); 1D interpolate lost float64/complex dtypes (#427); bandlimit and block_direct(radius=0) kept the direct beam on some grids (#506).

  • StructureFactorArray.get_projected_potential honours sampling= and an integer gpts and IndexedDiffractionPatterns.positions_dict is complete (#456).

  • No longer raising: Images.scan_noise, apply_ctf at a second energy, number - Images, lazy gaussian_filter with a constant boundary, lazy normalize, single-point GridScan, noisy near-orthogonal Potential cells, tilted eager Bloch exit waves with scalar thickness, IndexedDiffractionPatterns.sort(), core loss on 16 or 17 pixel grids, AnnularDetector over a LineScan with an extra ensemble axis, GPAW 26 calculators, frozen-phonon GPAWPotential, ensemble backscattering, A/B/C centring, graphene repeated along c, and numba typing on some numba/numpy pairings.

  • show(overlay=...) of an ensemble that has no base axes, such as the integrated core-loss measurements of the core-loss tutorial, gave more line labels than lines, and an ensemble of complex lines fewer. Changed: behaviour#

  • abTEM reads ~/.config/abtem and $ABTEM_CONFIG as well as dask’s configuration files (#416); 1.0.10 ignored them. A leftover cupy.fft-cache-size: 0 MB there now disables the cuFFT plan cache.

  • cupy.fft-cache-size, ignored in 1.0.10, is read and defaults to auto (25 % of the device memory per thread); 0 MB disables the cache, -1 removes the bound, and a plan larger than the bound runs uncached with a warning.
